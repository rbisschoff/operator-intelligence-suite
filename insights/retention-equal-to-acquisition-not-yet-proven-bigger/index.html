Retention Equal to Acquisition - Not Yet Proven Bigger

2026-09-27

The evidence supports treating owner retention as carrying the same commercial weight as owner acquisition - a portfolio that keeps adding properties while losing existing owners isn't really growing, it just looks like it is. It doesn't yet support the stronger claim that retention has become more decisive than acquisition specifically because of shorter booking windows and unmet pandemic-era return expectations - that particular mechanism rests on one practitioner's account only.

One practitioner ties owner churn directly to shrinking booking windows and owners still expecting 2021-level returns, and her fix is proactive, unscripted contact - the weekly call before there's a problem to report. A separate piece backs the underlying economics independently: chasing new owners while losing current ones just masks flat or declining underlying performance, though it doesn't mention booking windows or pandemic expectations at all, so it's confirming the general priority, not the specific mechanism. Where the pieces genuinely connect is on the how - another source on automated operations warns that owner trust erodes fast the moment communication starts feeling robotic rather than human, which lines up with the case for a real phone call over a system-generated update. One attached item about owners getting locked into an all-in-one platform due to switching costs doesn't really speak to this thesis - that's a technology lock-in problem, not an owner-relationship one - so I'd set it aside here and flag that the booking-window-and-expectations causal story is still just one operator's read on the market, not something backed by data yet.
